How the projection works

The engine rebuilds every defense's identity from every NFL team box score since the year you load (via nflverse). Each team tracks per-game rates of: points allowed, sacks, takeaways forced, giveaways, defensive/ST touchdowns, blocked kicks, and how often their offense gets sacked or turns it over — all split by home/away, exponentially weighted toward recent games, and shrunk toward league averages so small samples don't lie to you.

A matchup projection combines offense quality vs. defense vulnerability at that venue into an expected opponent score, then Monte-Carlo simulates ~1,500 outcomes per defense: opponent point totals are drawn (which set the points-allowed tier) and sacks/turnovers/defensive scores arrive as Poisson events. The average across simulations — not just the mode — is what ranks the board, because tier scoring is lumpy and upside matters for streamers.

Every team also carries a pressure rate — QB hits + sacks per opposing dropback — shown as pass-rush context (the ⚡ chip), as a sortable Multi-Year Explorer column, and inside opponent logs next to how much of the heat their QB actually lets become sacks (low conversion ≈ scramble escape artist or a soft rush). Pressure enters the projections where testing says it belongs: the pressure matchup nudges expected opponent scoring (walk-forward validated — points-ranking ρ .2765→.2811, weekly streamer lift +2.68→+2.81), while raw recent sack rates stay in charge of expected sacks — routing sack counts through hit totals diluted rather than helped, and Explorer logs show why: much of what hits know, late-season sack trends already figured out.

Optional weather: outdoor games in the next ~9 days get wind/rain adjustments pulled live from Open-Meteo (heavy wind/rain suppress expected scoring → boosts defenses).

All parameters were locked by walk-forward backtesting only on data the model could legitimately have known before each week (see the Backtest tab). It does not know injuries, QB changes, coaching tendencies or motivation — treat rankings as strong priors, not gospel.
